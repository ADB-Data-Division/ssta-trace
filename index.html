Questions and answers

What do I get from the service?

You choose a place and a date. The service finds clear Sentinel-2 images (10 m pixels) from around that date and makes a super-resolved image with 2.5 m pixels, in red, green, blue and near-infrared.

  • Stage 1 combines up to 8 Sentinel-2 images of the same place into one sharper image.
  • Stage 2 refines the stage 1 image with a diffusion model. It looks sharper, and more of its fine detail is generated by the model.
Do I need to write code?

No. Make an image runs in the browser. The notebooks run in Google Colab with nothing to install: you run the cells from top to bottom. If you do code, the Python client gives you the same service in a few lines.

How do I get access, and how long does it last?

Type your email address on the Make an image page or in a notebook. The service makes a key for that address, and the same address always gets the same key back. Nothing is sent to your inbox.

A workshop key allows areas up to 5 km × 5 km and has a budget of USD 3. Keys from the workshop sign-up stop working on 28 November 2026 at 08:00 Manila time. If you want to continue after that, come to the office hours.

Is the detail in the image real?

Not all of it. Stage 1 is built from several real Sentinel-2 images of the place. Stage 2 adds detail that looks plausible for this kind of place: the outline of a large building is usually right, the texture of a roof may be invented.

So treat fine detail as a good guess, not a measurement. Before a decision depends on it, test the images for your task against a real high-resolution image or a field visit. Where you need measured values, for example a vegetation index, use stage 1 or the Sentinel-2 image.

How do I open the image in QGIS?
  1. On the image page, download stage2_sr.tif (or copy its link).
  2. Drag the file into QGIS. For a link: Layer → Add Layer → Add Raster Layer, source type “Protocol: HTTP(S)”, paste the link.
  3. If the image looks dark: Layer Properties → Symbology, set Min 0 and Max 3000 for the red, green and blue band. Bands 1 to 4 are red, green, blue and near-infrared.

job = sr.job("the id of your image")

job.download("results/")              # all files
What do the warnings mean?

A warning never stops an image. It tells you where to look twice.

  • water: much of the area is water. Super-resolution removes detail in shallow water, so use the Sentinel-2 image there.
  • low_diversity: fewer than 4 different clear Sentinel-2 images were found for part of the area. The result can be less sharp there. A longer search period or a higher cloud limit helps.
  • nodata: part of the area has no clear image and stays empty.
  • haze: the Sentinel-2 images look hazy. Try another date.
  • missing_tiles: part of the area could not be processed. Run it again.
May I use the images in a report or a presentation?

Yes. Say that it is a super-resolved (AI-enhanced) Sentinel-2 image, so that nobody takes it for a real high-resolution photo, and add the credit “Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data” with the year of the image.

A page or a notebook does not load on my office network. What now?

Some office networks block this site, Google Colab or the image service. Try mobile data or another network. The tracing exercise also exists as a Colab notebook. If nothing works, follow on a teammate's screen and tell the workshop team.

What happens to what I type on these pages?
  • Ideas and projects (text, name, affiliation) are stored in a Google Sheet of the workshop team, and everyone with the link can read them on the boards.
  • Your email address on the image page goes only to the image service, which stores it with your key. Your key is kept in your own browser.
  • The tracing exercise stores your lines and scores without a name.
